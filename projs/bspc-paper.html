Summary

Manual and semi-automated lumbar measurements vary between observers, particularly when end-plate deformity or osteophytes alter the landmarks used for Meyerding grading and the modified Cobb method. This paper builds a measurement pipeline on mid-sagittal lumbar MRI and tests two classification rules that do not depend on those end-plate corners.

Of 515 symptomatic examinations in the Sudirman collection, 514 mid-sagittal slices were labelled for L1–L5 and S1. A radiologist selected the slice; spinal surgeons checked the labels. Pixel spacing was retained so that distances could be reported in millimetres. The labels and the measurements made on them were released as a composite set.

Segmentation was compared between a morphological procedure, VBSeg, and encoder–decoder networks trained by fine-tuning (360 images for training, 154 for testing; Adadelta, cross-entropy, 50 epochs). ResNet-UNet was the strongest of the networks tested. Its masks, and separately the manual labels, were then used to compute lumbar height, intervertebral distances, vertebral-body dimensions, the lumbar lordotic angle and the lumbosacral angle.

Spondylolisthesis was called from the angular deviation of vertebral centroids, which records both anterior and posterior slip. Lumbar lordosis was classed as reduced, within range, or increased from the area enclosed by the centroid curve, using the clinical bounds of 39° and 53°. The stated purpose is to check a manual reading, not to replace it.

The correlation of 0.979 is between measurements on the manual labels and the surgeon’s readings. It is not the agreement of the network masks with the surgeon. On the same 50 examinations, lumbosacral angle and both lumbar-height definitions remained highly correlated for the label–surgeon comparison (R = 0.951–0.997). Agreement fell once the masks came from ResNet-UNet.
