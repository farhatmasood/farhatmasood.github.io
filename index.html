About

I am a Doctoral Researcher specializing in Computer Vision and Medical Image Analysis. Grounded in a decade of clinical biomedical engineering experience, my research bridges the gap between Vision-Language Foundation Models and Radiological Workflows, specifically targeting the automation of lumbar spine MRI interpretation.

My current work addresses the computational bottlenecks of deploying Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) in clinical settings. I develop parameter-efficient, prompt-calibrated architectures—leveraging Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) and hierarchical attention mechanisms (HiTAD)—to compress massive visual encoders. Furthermore, I formulate novel optimization strategies, such as Weighted Ensemble Loss with Diversity Enhancement (WELDE), to resolve severe class imbalances in multi-label spinal disorder detection, ensuring edge-deployable solutions with high clinical fidelity.

From Pixels to Reports: Rethinking Clinical Diagnostics

Compressing massive foundation models into edge-deployable, multi-task architectures.

Can we distill the reasoning power of massive Vision-Language Models into a framework lean enough for consumer-grade clinical hardware?

PC-RadNet proves we can. By applying Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) — formalized as h = W0x + (α/r)BAx — to a frozen DINOv2 visual encoder, the model conditions entirely on free-text clinical prompts, bypassing the need for costly dense spatial annotations.

To resolve the persistent challenge of overlapping, highly imbalanced spinal pathologies, this architecture integrates the HiTAD framework. It penalizes correlated ensemble errors and applies a dynamically weighted focal loss via the WELDE objective:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{WELDE} = \sum_{m=1}^{M} \omega_m \mathcal{L}_{Focal}(y, \hat{y}_m) + \gamma \sum_{i \neq j} \mathcal{D}_{KL}(\mathcal{P}_{\theta_i} \parallel \mathcal{P}_{\theta_j}) $$

The Impact

A direct, reproducible pathway generating RadLex-compliant structured reports from raw MRI sequences. Executing in just 39 ms/image with a peak VRAM of 0.63 GB, this architecture shatters the computational bottlenecks of modern medical AI.

PC-RadNet
2026
Prompt-Calibrated, Parameter-Efficient Foundation Model for Comprehensive Multi-Task Segmentation, Clinical Assessment and Structured Report Generation in Lumbar Spine MRI
Codebase & Model Weights

Unifies five interdependent radiological tasks—anatomical segmentation, Pfirrmann grading, intervertebral disc height quantification, multi-label pathology detection, and structured report generation—within a single parameter-efficient architecture. Built on a frozen DINOv2 ViT-B/14 visual encoder coupled with a four-stage ConvTranspose decoder, the model requires no spatial annotations at inference, conditioning solely on free-text clinical prompts. Executes the full pipeline in 39 ms per image with a minimal footprint of 0.63 GB peak VRAM on consumer-grade hardware, utilizing a Spine Domain Adapter and LoRA for highly efficient distillation.

Review Paper
2026
Vision-Language Foundation Models for Lumbar Spine Diagnostics: A Systematic Review of Architectures, Knowledge Distillation and Clinical Deployment Pathways
Systematic Review & Meta-Analysis

A comprehensive synthesis of 165 peer-reviewed studies (2015–2026) evaluating the integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and LLMs in spine diagnostics. Introduces a unified mathematical framework detailing self-attention, contrastive pre-training, and knowledge distillation objectives. Features a random-effects meta-analysis of 14 spine segmentation pipelines establishing a pooled vertebral Dice coefficient of 0.897. Concludes by identifying six critical research gaps and proposing a resource-stratified, three-tier deployment roadmap to guide the clinical translation of volumetric multi-sequence processing models.

LSMA-PQR

Lumbar Spine Multi-view Annotations with Pfirrmann grading, Quantitative measurements and structured Radiological reports
Dataset & Benchmark

A clinically validated lumbar spine MRI dataset comprising 515 patients with dual-plane, dual-sequence imaging (4,120 total images) and comprehensive multi-modal annotations. Provides pixel-level segmentation masks for seven anatomical structures in multiple interoperable formats (YOLO polygon, semantic) with verified cross-format spatial consistency (mean IoU = 0.994). Includes 1,545 Pfirrmann degeneration grades, intervertebral disc height measurements, and structured radiological reports derived from 515 free-text documents.

WELDE
2025
Weighted Ensemble Loss with Diversity Enhancement for imbalanced medical image classification
Method & Pipeline

Addresses long-tailed class imbalance in medical image classification by combining four complementary loss functions — Cross-Entropy, Focal Loss, Class-Balanced Loss, and LDAM — via per-head adapter projections, EMA-based normalisation, and learnable adaptive weighting with a relaxed sum-to-one penalty. Validated on spinal disorder classification and the DermaMNIST skin lesion benchmark.

Repository
HiTAD
2025
A clinically validated hierarchical two-stage attention-enhanced architecture for multi-label overlapping spinal disorder detection
Detection Framework

Automated YOLO-based pipeline for precise detection of six overlapping spinal disorders (DDD, IVD, SS, Spondylolisthesis, LDB, TDB) in MRI. Achieves 84.90% mAP₅₀₋₉₅ on internal dataset (1,038 images, 8,991 annotations) and 79.08% on external cross-validation (RSNA 2024 derivative). Inference at 0.48s per scan — 180× faster than expert clinicians. Clinically validated by 2 radiologists and 1 spinal surgeon across 350 images.
